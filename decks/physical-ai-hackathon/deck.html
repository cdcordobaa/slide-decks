This is the robot's proposal when the CO2 pressure drops: close V-3, because CO2 pools low and displaces oxygen. The G1's hands have no fingers, so that grip is something we had to build, and we want to be clear about it. [Approve] What is simulated is the grip itself: when a palm is within a centimetre of the rim, a constraint switches on. Everything else is physics: hinge friction decides whether the valve turns, so one hand gets about 20 degrees and two hands close it in two turns; the force goes through the arms and the legs keep balance; and the cart, the tray and the e-stop move only when the robot actually pushes them.

The language model is the heavy one: its agent process, with Whisper, holds almost eleven gigabytes of GPU memory, two thirds of it; the weights alone are about eight and a half, an estimate; and it is busy about a fifth of the time with its looks. One thing we learned: the container's own memory count said 11 gigabytes, but the GPU processes alone hold almost 16, over the 15 gigabyte limit, because CPU and GPU share the same memory. Checkpoint 7 · Muster point · Lessons and the ask

Physics, not animation.
Our first valve close was an animation. We rebuilt every action on contact physics.

Long runs find bugs.

Say what is simulated.
